# Career Timeline

## 2020–2021: Learning by building

I started programming with Python in school, including ANA, a simple voice-command assistant built with speech recognition. During my first year of BCA at GLS University, I explored frontend development, AI/ML theory, and Node.js before finding the direction that stuck: blockchain systems.

## 2022: Blockchain and first on-chain builds

I discovered blockchain during BCA and began learning through documentation, technical articles, tutorials, and hands-on experimentation. I built an NFT-minting DApp, wrote its Solidity contracts, deployed it on Ropsten, and hosted the application on Vercel.

My earliest hackathons were difficult. I often tried to build too much alone and struggled to integrate smart contracts into complete products. That period taught me to scope more carefully, collaborate, and make the underlying technology demonstrable.

## 2023: Hackathons and technical ownership

I became more comfortable building blockchain applications in teams and increasingly owned the technical direction: smart-contract design, backend architecture, and integration.

- **Cyber Shadez:** First place for Sandesh, a decentralized communications system.
- **DU Hacks:** Won the Polygon track for Save Tiger, an ERC-721 fundraising DApp built with Next.js and ethers.js.
- **Cyber Shadez:** Second place for an account-abstraction project using Solidity, Next.js, Prisma, and Supabase.

## 2024: From building protocols to securing them

After completing a BCA with 84%, I began an MCA at Jain University. The WazirX hack pushed me to look beyond how blockchain systems are built and toward how they fail.

My early security-research period was difficult: roughly 15–20 reports were rejected, invalid, or out of scope. It taught me the difference between a suspicious observation and a valid, reproducible vulnerability with clear impact.

## 2025: Full-time blockchain security research

In January 2025, I moved into blockchain security research full-time. I audited protocols, participated in public competitions, studied EVM internals and DeFi mechanisms, developed PoCs, and strengthened my vulnerability-research methodology.

This work produced **18 validated findings across 8 public audit competitions**, including **3rd out of 723** in Cantina's Kuru audit. Alongside public competitions, I also completed private reviews under NDA.

## 2025–2026: Security engineering with Shim

Alongside security research, I began co-building **Shim**, a Go-based security layer for npm, pnpm, and yarn. It intercepts package-manager activity and evaluates install risk through scanning, lifecycle-script analysis, local rules, policies, caching, and daemon-based tooling.

Shim extends my security work beyond smart contracts into developer environments and software supply-chain risk.

## 2026–present: Cryptography and independent research

I am now studying zero-knowledge proofs and deeper cryptography. I am writing *Intro to ZK Math*, a beginner-oriented resource for the mathematics behind ZK systems, and developing an independent research project on a serverless communications system.

The goal is to continue moving deeper: from application-level vulnerabilities into the systems, cryptography, and research questions underneath them.

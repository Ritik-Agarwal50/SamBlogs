# Career Timeline

## 2025–present: Security research and security engineering

In January 2025, I moved into blockchain security research full-time. Since then I have reported **18 validated findings across 8 public audit competitions**, with a standout result of **3rd out of 723** in Cantina's Kuru audit. Alongside public competitions, I have completed private reviews under NDA.

Since mid-2025, I have also co-built **Shim**, a Go-based security layer for npm, pnpm, and yarn. It intercepts package-manager activity and evaluates package-install risk through scanning, lifecycle-script analysis, local rules, policies, caching, and daemon-based tooling. We are preparing v1 for an open-source release.

In 2026, I began studying zero-knowledge proofs and deeper cryptography. I am writing *Intro to ZK Math*, a personal beginner resource, and developing a research paper on a server-less communications system.

## 2024: From building protocols to securing them

After completing a BCA with 84%, I began an MCA at Jain University. The WazirX hack pushed me to look beyond how blockchain systems are built and toward how they fail. My early security-research period was difficult: roughly 15–20 reports were rejected, invalid, or out of scope. It taught me the gap between a suspicious observation and a valid, reproducible vulnerability with clear impact.

## 2022–2023: Solidity, DApps, and hackathons

I discovered blockchain during BCA and built an NFT-minting DApp, writing and deploying Solidity contracts on Ropsten. After several early hackathon failures, I learned to collaborate, scope projects, and communicate technical work.

- **Cyber Shadez:** First place for Sandesh, a decentralized communications system; second place the following year for an account-abstraction project.
- **DU Hacks:** Won the Polygon track for Save Tiger, an ERC-721 fundraising DApp built with Next.js and ethers.js.
- **Technical direction:** Took increasing responsibility for smart-contract design, backend architecture, and product integration.

## 2020–2021: Learning by building

I started programming with Python in school, including ANA, a simple voice-command assistant built with speech recognition. During my first year of BCA at GLS University, I explored frontend development, AI/ML theory, and Node.js before finding the direction that stuck: blockchain systems.
